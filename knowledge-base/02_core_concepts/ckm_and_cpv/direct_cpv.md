# Direct CP Violation

## One-sentence definition
CP violation that occurs directly in the decay amplitude, without involving mixing.

## Physical picture
Different decay rates for particle and antiparticle: Γ(B → f) ≠ Γ(B̄ → f̄)

## Mathematical expression
A_CP = (Γ(B → f) - Γ(B̄ → f̄)) / (Γ(B → f) + Γ(B̄ → f̄))

Requires interference between amplitudes with different weak and strong phases.

## Where it appears in experiment
- B → Kπ decays (tree vs penguin interference)
- Charm decays D → KK, ππ
- Baryon decays Λ_b → pK

## Common confusions
- **Not the same as mixing-induced CPV** - direct CPV doesn't need B⁰-B̄⁰ oscillation
- **Requires both weak and strong phase difference** - one alone is not enough
- **Detector asymmetry can fake it** - production/detection asymmetries must be controlled

## Comparison with similar concepts
- **vs Mixing-induced CPV**: needs B⁰-B̄⁰ mixing, time-dependent
- **vs Indirect CPV**: occurs in mixing itself (ε_K in kaon system)

## Example
B⁺ → K⁺π⁰: A_CP ≈ +5% (tree-penguin interference)

## Can I explain it without formulas?
Particle and antiparticle decay at different rates to the same final state, because quantum interference between different decay paths has opposite signs for particle vs antiparticle.
